For PK ❤️

Some memories don't ask permission to come back. They just arrive, quietly, the way you did — back in 2018, on a day I didn't know would matter until every day after it did. I still remember that day with a clarity I can't explain. The way the air felt. The way I couldn't stop smiling for no reason I was willing to admit.

I remember the first treat you gave me at Bottoli, too — such a small thing, really. A table, a plate, a little laughter between two people who didn't yet know what they were starting. But that's the strange thing about small memories: they don't ask to be important. They just quietly become priceless, long after the moment has passed, when you finally understand what you were living through.

I think about the version of us that could have stayed. If we had put down our pride, just once, at the right moment instead of the wrong one, maybe we'd still be finding our way back to each other instead of finding our way through everything else. Pride is a strange thing to lose someone over. It feels like protection at the time. It only feels like a mistake later.

And I think about COVID — that strange, still year the whole world seemed to pause. I've wondered, more than once, what might have happened if you'd given me a second chance in the middle of all that silence. Maybe life would have turned out differently. Maybe we'd have had all that quiet time to simply choose each other again.

Sometimes, even now, I catch myself imagining another ending for us — one where a conversation goes differently, where someone stays five more minutes, where neither of us walks away. I don't do it to live in the past. I do it because some stories feel unfinished, even when they're over.

I don't blame you. I want to say that clearly, because I mean it. Life pulls people in directions no one plans for, and you did what felt right to you at the time, just as I did what felt right to me. I'm not angry. I never really was. I simply miss what we could have been — the version of us that never got the chance to exist.

Thank you for every memory, even the ones that ache a little now. Thank you for 2018, for Bottoli, for every ordinary afternoon that I only recognized as extraordinary once it was gone. You gave me a season of my life I wouldn't erase even if I could. I hope, wherever you are now, that you're happy — genuinely, quietly happy, in the way that outlasts any one person or any one story.

"If destiny ever lets our paths cross again... maybe this time we won't let each other go."

Some stories never truly end.
They simply become memories.
